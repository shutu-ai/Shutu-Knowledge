## Validation classification

**WEAK.**

Import discovery and completeness now pass, but semantic/data-model capability still cannot be certified:

- A representative 20-workbook embedding subset began successfully, but the local ONNX runtime repeatedly consumed about 3.2 GB and exhibited runaway CPU. This persisted after bounded process retirement, explicit tensor disposal, and a 512-token embedding cap.
- Only the five smallest embedded workbooks remained usable before the runtime gate.
- Search over the populated corpus exceeded the synchronous deadline on several subset queries; some hybrid searches also timed out waiting on the local model.
- The quality scorer initially marked non-PDF native extraction as low quality. That defect was fixed and covered by regression, but existing records require another indexing pass to refresh quality metadata.
- The full 150-question evaluation was stopped rather than producing misleading semantic or data-model scores.

## Correctness fixes made during validation

- `.xlsm` is now parsed by the native OOXML structured workbook path. Previously it was discoverable but rejected as unsupported.
- Large merged-cell workbooks no longer scan every merge for every cell; a merged-cell index prevents quadratic behavior.
- Document IR parent/child construction no longer repeatedly scans the full node list.
- Chunk-to-node matching and structural context now use indexed lookups instead of full IR scans per chunk.
- Large document IR node writes and cleanup of unpublished generations are transaction-batched instead of exceeding a 30-second write deadline.
- The splitter no longer advances by one rune when a preferred cut is shorter than the overlap, preventing extreme chunk creep.
- Non-PDF native extraction no longer inherits the PDF fragmentation health default, so structured Excel extraction is no longer misclassified as low quality.

Regression fixtures cover `.xlsm`, large merged-cell metadata, large IR tree binding, short-cut chunk windowing, and non-PDF quality classification.

### Sanitized retrieval evidence

- With managed runtime disabled to isolate lexical search, a five-legacy-workbook subset returned hits for 6 of 10 generic queries and zero for 4; all calls completed quickly.
- The legacy `.xls` subset exposed only logical text anchors, not workbook/sheet/cell anchors.
- A smallest-OOXML workbook exposed xlsx sheet anchors on all hits in a four-query smoke; source-anchor granularity to cell range was not observed at search-hit level.
- These are smoke results, not the required 150-question evaluation.

## Candidate gaps

1. The managed local embedding runtime is not production-safe for this corpus: even with process retirement, tensor disposal, and a 512-token cap, it consumes ~3.2 GB and shows runaway CPU.
2. Synchronous retrieval can exceed its deadline on the populated corpus, especially when model work competes with lexical search.
3. Existing quality metadata needs a refreshed indexing pass after the non-PDF quality-scoring fix.
4. Semantic field discovery, cross-workbook reasoning, join recommendation, and data-model readiness remain unproven.

## Conclusion

The corpus is not yet validated as data-model knowledge. Although structured import completeness now passes, the AI is not yet demonstrably operating beyond searchable cells with anchors. The stop rule was applied before high-level QA to avoid overstating capability.

# v0.6.3 runtime resolution and completed semantic QA

## Runtime blocker resolution

The v0.6.2 stop was caused by unstable local model runtime, not by a workbook-import regression. The v0.6.3 candidate replaced the oversized Qwen3 embedding path with a pinned MiniLM model, bounded ONNX threading and process retirement, primary-key vector persistence, top-K vector reads, a q4 reranker, and a bounded top-K rerank window. The final corpus state has **154,475 / 154,475** embedded active chunks. The largest workbook has **64,268 / 64,268** vectors and atomically completed a new generation.

The completed 100-query-per-mode runtime benchmark had **0 errors and 0 timeouts** for BM25, vector, hybrid, and hybrid+reranker. Hybrid + reranker p50/p95 was **10.23 s / 12.54 s**, and the reranker applied in **100 / 100** eligible searches. These are runtime improvements, not proof of semantic quality.

## Completed QA outcome

The previously stopped 150-question evaluation was completed with 50 exact, 30 semantic reverse, 30 cross-workbook, 20 analysis, and 20 join/data-model questions. All 150 searches completed in BM25, hybrid, and hybrid+reranker modes.

Exact lookup remained strongest: BM25 found target evidence in 50/50, while hybrid and hybrid+reranker found 46/50. Semantic reverse lookup remained at only 7/30 in every mode. Cross-workbook concept evidence remained 0/30. Analysis was 2-3/20. Join/data-model evidence was 3/20. Citation presence improved from 136/150 lexical to 142/150 with hybrid/rerank. No unsupported join claims were produced because the harness evaluated retrieval evidence rather than allowing generated join assertions.

## Final classification

**WEAK.**

This is a different failure boundary from v0.6.2:

- v0.6.2: import completed, but runtime instability stopped semantic QA.
- v0.6.3: runtime completed full-corpus embedding, search, reranking, and the 150-question QA, but semantic/data-model quality remains weak.

Therefore v0.6.3 must not be tagged or released until the semantic, cross-workbook, analysis, and join/data-model gaps are addressed and revalidated.
