## Validation classification

**WEAK.**

Most files import, but the evidence is insufficient to call the Excel corpus production-usable as structured data-model knowledge:

- The required structural sampling gates did not run because the import/quality gates did not pass.
- The large-workbook concurrent write/retry path remains a performance blocker.
- 51 of 58 records were marked low quality, indicating chunk noise or an unusable quality threshold.
- Local embedding and reranker were unavailable, so semantic discovery could not be measured.
- A seven-query lexical smoke was run only as a sanity check; the full 150-question evaluation was stopped.

## Correctness fixes made during validation

- `.xlsm` is now parsed by the native OOXML structured workbook path. Previously it was discoverable but rejected as unsupported.
- Large merged-cell workbooks no longer scan every merge for every cell; a merged-cell index prevents quadratic behavior.
- Document IR parent/child construction no longer repeatedly scans the full node list.
- Chunk-to-node matching and structural context now use indexed lookups instead of full IR scans per chunk.
- Large document IR node writes are transaction-batched instead of requiring all nodes in one 30-second write.
- The splitter no longer advances by one rune when a preferred cut is shorter than the overlap, preventing extreme chunk creep.

Regression fixtures cover `.xlsm`, large merged-cell metadata, large IR tree binding, and short-cut chunk windowing.

## Candidate gaps

1. Concurrent ingestion of a 560-sheet / 471k-node workbook still exceeds the bounded SQLite write deadline.
2. Low-quality classification is widespread across imported Excel files; chunk noise or quality scoring needs targeted diagnosis.
3. Local embedding/reranker readiness is required before semantic field discovery and data-model reasoning can be assessed.

## Conclusion

The corpus is not yet validated as data-model knowledge. Current behavior is closer to searchable cells with structured anchors than to reliable table/field/join reasoning. The stop rule was applied before high-level QA to avoid overstating capability.
